# Question banks

A bank is a **reservoir, not a script**. Pull 1–3 questions in naturally. If it ever feels like
they are doing you a favour by talking, it is too formal.

Every question below is in past-behaviour form. When you instantiate them, replace bracketed
slots with the real person, kid, tool or number they just named.

---

## Category skeleton (works for any persona)

Generate 3–6 per category, in this order. Each must ladder up to a hypothesis.

0. **Screener / fast facts** (2 min) — role, household or team shape, who controls the budget,
   who else has a veto.
1. **Context & current workflow** — *"Walk me through the last time you [did the task]."*
   *"Walk me through your whole workflow for X — what tools, what people, at each step?"*
   *"Where did it break last time?"*
   > Open broad unless the problem is a proven must-solve for this persona. Asking "what's the
   > biggest problem with the gym?" of someone who never goes manufactures a false positive.
   > Open with *"What are you working on right now?"* or *"What are the top three most painful
   > problems in your [work / week] right now?"* and zoom in only once **they** raise your area.
   > If they never raise it, that is your finding.
2. **Problem reality & frequency** — *"When did this last happen?"* *"How many times last
   week?"* *"What triggered it that time?"*
3. **Implications & stakes** — *"What were the consequences that time?"* *"What did it cost you
   in money, hours, or standing?"* *"What happened when you left it unfixed?"*
4. **Existing alternatives & the search test** — *"What else have you tried?"* *"How are you
   dealing with it right now?"* *"What do you love and hate about that?"* *"What does it cost?"*
   *"Have you searched for something better — what did you find?"* (Never having Googled it is
   gold: they do not care enough.)
5. **Money already spent** — *"What did the last thing you bought in this area cost?"* *"What
   are you paying for right now across tools and people?"* *"Whose budget was that?"* *"Who else
   had to approve it, and who could have killed it?"*
6. **Motivation** — *"Why do you bother doing it that way?"* *"What were you ultimately trying
   to get done?"*
7. **Digging into a feature request** — *"What did that let you do?"* *"How are you coping
   without it now?"* *"Walk me through the last time you needed it."*
8. **Digging into emotion** — any strong reaction: *"Tell me more about that."* *"That seems to
   really bug you — what makes it so awful?"* *"Why haven't you fixed it already?"*
9. **Commitment & advancement** — *"Can I sit in the next time this happens?"* *"Can we book a
   follow-up next week to go deeper?"* *"Who on your team hits this — can you introduce me this
   week?"* *"Can you put a deposit on the prototype?"* A real yes costs them something.
10. **Close** — *"Who else should I talk to?"* *"What should I have asked but didn't?"*

## Bank A — personal AI / agents for founders and operators

For the persona: *a founder or operator who already pays for AI tools and has tried to wire up
their own workflows.* Slots: `[tool]`, `[inbox]`, `[person]`.

**Time and attention**
- What are you working on right now?
- What were the three most painful problems in your week last week?
- Walk me through how yesterday actually went, first thing to last thing.
- When you woke up yesterday, how did you decide what to do first?
- What did you drop last week that you meant to do?

**Decisions and memory**
- Walk me through the last real decision you made — who did you consult, what did you write down?
- What was the last mistake you actually changed a process over?
- The last time you forgot something important — what was it, and what happened?
- What did you do to stop it happening again? Did that stick?

**Inboxes and capture**
- Which inboxes did you open yesterday, in order?
- What is the last message you left unanswered for more than three days? Why that one?
- Do you record or take notes? Show me the last note you took — did you ever reopen it?
- Where did the last useful thought you had end up?

**Relationships**
- When did you last reach out to a friend or family member you had been meaning to contact?
- What did you use to remember to do it?
- What was the last real conflict with your partner or co-founder, and how did it resolve?
- What have you tried to stay closer to people — apps, shared calendars, standing calls? Which
  ones are dead now, and what killed them?

**Agents and tooling (money already spent)**
- Which agents or AI tools have you actually paid for? What does each cost per month?
- Which one did you cancel, and what killed it?
- Walk me through the last workflow you built yourself. How long did it survive?
- What broke the last time an agent did something on your behalf?
- What did you spend on AI last month, in total, across everything?

**Wearables and form factor**
- What are you wearing right now, and what made you start?
- Tell me about a wearable you stopped using — how long did it last and what killed it?
- Where on your body have you refused to put something, and why?
- When did you last take it off and not put it back on?

**Trust and privacy (ask for incidents, not positions)**
- Have you ever refused to install something, or deleted it, over privacy? Which one, and what
  triggered it?
- When did an AI last get something wrong in front of you or a colleague? What did you do after?
- What data have you deliberately kept out of a tool? What happened the last time you almost
  pasted it in?

## Bank B — parents and kids' devices

For the persona: *a parent of a 5–12 year old who has already bought at least one device or
tracker for the child.* Slots: `[kid]`, `[device]`.

> Never mention the product, the category, or AI. You are "curious how families handle screens
> and staying in touch." Read the pitch rule in `ground-rules.md` first.

**0. Screener** — how many kids, ages and genders; who is home when; nanny, after-school care,
grandparents; city or suburb; who makes purchase decisions for the kids.

**1. A day in the life**
- Walk me through yesterday with [kid], wake-up to bedtime.
- How much one-on-one time did you get with [kid] last week? How do you know?
- What did [kid] do between school pickup and dinner yesterday? Who arranged that?
- Which part of the day with them do you dread? When it happened this week, what did you do?
- What do you outsource — care, activities, a screen as a babysitter? What does each cost?
- What have you been meaning to do with [kid] and haven't? What stopped you?

**2. Device timeline** — the single highest-yield sequence; it produced the age window, the
purchase trigger and the abandonment graveyard in one pass.
- Go kid by kid: when did each one get their first tablet, watch, phone — and **what was
  happening that made you buy it?** (Listen for the trigger. On a real call it was separation —
  friends' houses and sports fields at about seven — not a birthday.)
- How long did each one actually get used? What happened when it stopped?
- When it broke or got abandoned, what did you do — replace, upgrade, or give up?
- Who charges [kid]'s devices — you or them? What happened the last time one died?
- Did you ever have to force them to wear or use something? Tell me about the last fight over it.
- When did friends start influencing what [kid] wants to own? Tell me about that moment.

**3. Screens (past behaviour only)**
- Which devices can [kid] use? Which are *theirs*?
- The last time you handed them a screen to buy yourself a break — what was going on?
- Hours per day, honestly? How do you know — when did you last open the screen-time report, and
  what did you do afterwards? (Checked-but-did-nothing is a finding.)
- Which rules have you tried — timers, app locks, no screens at dinner? Which are dead now, and
  what killed them?
- Where do the parental controls fail you? Give me the last example.
- What was the last app request you rejected, and why? How many do you get a week?
- Biggest screen fight in the last month — walk me through it. How did it end?
- Who is more against screens, you or your partner? What was the actual argument in your house?

**4. Learning and enrichment spend**
- What is [kid] enrolled in right now — classes, tutoring, sports, camps, apps? Go down the
  list: **what does each cost per month?**
- The last learning-related thing you bought — what triggered it, and where did you hear about it?
- What did you cancel or let lapse in the past year? Why?
- Which "educational" apps do they have? Do they open them unprompted, or only when made to?
- What have you personally made, printed or invented to teach or entertain [kid]? (DIY effort is
  the strongest demand evidence there is — one parent hand-wrote puzzle scavenger hunts for hours.)

**5. Communication, safety, and the blindness probe**
- When [kid] is not with you, how do you reach them? Walk me through the last time you needed to.
- Do you check their location? How often — and **what is the feeling that drives that action?**
- Do they have a phone, watch or tracker? **What was the specific incident that made you buy it?**
  What does it cost per month?
- The last time [kid] came home upset and wouldn't say why — how did you eventually find out?
- Has anything with a teacher, coach or sitter ever made you uneasy? What did you change or buy
  afterwards?
- Do you have cameras at home? When did you last actually review the footage?

**6. The gadget graveyard**
- Has [kid] ever had a wearable — a fitness band, a kid smartwatch, even a Tamagotchi?
- The one they stopped using: how long did it last, and what killed it — lost, broken, bored,
  battery, or stigma?
- Do *you* wear one? What made you start, and what made you stop?
- The last toy they begged for and abandoned within a month — what was it?

**7. Kid's social life (the viral-loop test)**
- When did [kid] last have a friend over? How did it get arranged — who talked to whom?
- How often do they see friends outside school? Free play or scheduled?
- How do they keep in touch with friends they don't see? On what device?
- Have they used a kids' messaging app or device? How many of their friends have it, and how did
  those families hear about it? (On a real call the answer was parent-to-parent evangelism, which
  is a growth channel, not a feature.)

**8. AI attitudes (ask late, never lead)**
- Has [kid] ever talked to a voice assistant or chatbot? What was the last thing they asked?
- How many times a day does someone in the house talk to one? Count it.
- Has an AI ever got something wrong in front of your kid? What happened after?
- Have *you* used AI for anything parenting-related? When was the last time?
- Have you ever returned or refused to buy something over kid-privacy? Which product?
- Has anyone in the family objected to a camera or mic in the house? What happened?

**9. Wish probes — hypothetical zone, always anchored**
- What do you wish you could do more of for [kid]? → **What have you tried? What stopped you?**
- Hardest part of raising them right now? → When did that last bite you? → How are you handling
  it today? → What does that cost you in money or hours?
- Save "what product do you wish existed?" for last, and treat the answer as a pointer to a
  problem, never a spec. Always follow with *"why that?"*

**10. Close — the ask test**
- What was your last $100+ purchase for [kid], and how did that decision actually happen — who
  raised it, who vetoed, how long did it take?
- Which two or three parents do you know who obsess about this? Will you introduce me this week?
  (Ask for the under-represented half of your sample by name.)
- Only if real pain surfaced: *"Can I show you a rough prototype in a few weeks?"* Their yes or
  no is data. A deposit later is better data.
